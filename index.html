Abstract

Latent world models are inherently strong encoders that transform image pixel to latent embedding, yet existing world models still rely on online trajectory optimization for action planning: for every state–goal pair, an iterative optimizer is run from scratch to search for optimal action sequences, treating the world model as a black-box simulator. This approach pays the full iterative optimization cost anew at every replanning step and reuses no planning experience across queries. In this work, we ask whether planning itself can be amortized once a latent world model has been learned. We present LeFlow, which learns a reusable latent trajectory prior operating directly in the latent dynamics space from the world model. LeFlow recasts planning as conditional latent trajectory generation: a rectified-flow model imagines a future latent path between the current and goal embeddings, an inverse dynamics decoder turns latent transitions into action chunks, and the frozen world model verifies each candidate by autoregressive rollout. Across four major goal-conditioned pixel-control benchmarks, LeFlow replaces iterative action-space optimization with amortized latent planning and fixed-budget rollout selection, achieving consistent success-rate gains with an order-of-magnitude reduction in planning time. Our results argue that latent world models should support not only prediction but reusable planning priors.

Method

Plan in latent state space, decode actions afterwards, verify with the world model.

World models such as LeWM plan with the Cross-Entropy Method: for every state–goal pair they search over raw action sequences from scratch, querying the world model thousands of times per replanning step and reusing nothing across queries. Action sequences are low-level and highly multimodal, since many of them realize nearly the same state trajectory. Latent state trajectories are smoother and already organized by the world model's encoder, so LeFlow learns its planning prior in that space instead.

1

Latent flow planner

A conditional rectified-flow model generates the interior of a latent path whose endpoints are clamped to $z_{\text{start}}$ and $z_{\text{goal}}$. Its near-straight transport needs only 16 Euler steps, and $N{=}64$ candidates are sampled in parallel.

2

Inverse dynamics decoder

A lightweight MLP maps each latent transition, together with its displacement, to an executable action chunk: $a_t = g_\omega([z_t,\, z_{t+1},\, z_{t+1}-z_t])$. The planner decides where to go; the decoder decides how.

3

Rollout reranking

Each decoded action sequence is rolled out through the frozen LeWM predictor. The candidate whose actual rollout lands closest to the goal is executed. This projects generated paths back onto the latent manifold the world model can actually control.

Training objective

LeWM stays frozen. Only the flow planner $v_\psi$ and the decoder $g_\omega$ are trained, jointly, on offline trajectories:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} = \mathbb{E}\,\big\lVert \mathrm{pred}_\phi(z_t, \hat a_t) - z_{t+1} \big\rVert_2^2$$

The consistency term decodes each generated transition, rolls it one step through the frozen predictor and penalizes the gap. This distills the world model's dynamics into the planner at training time, while reranking selects among samples at inference. A small weight ($\lambda_{\text{cons}}{=}0.1$) is enough; a large one collapses diversity onto the predictor's one-step behavior.
